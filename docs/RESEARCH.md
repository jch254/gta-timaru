# Research notes: Christchurch, Timaru and SH1, c. 1995

Compiled from English Wikipedia, Christchurch City Libraries' chronology, WuHoo Timaru and present-day OpenStreetMap street names, and extended in November 2026 by a web research pass whose full notes, with a link and a verbatim quote for every item, are in `docs/research/` (nine files; see the index at the end of this page). Every item below either carries a source link, points to the numbered research file that holds its link and quote, or is marked **(unverified)**. Unverified items could not be confirmed on a page that was actually opened; treat them as plausible rather than certain.

## Christchurch (pre-earthquake)

- **Four avenues**: Bealey (N), Fitzgerald (E), Moorhouse (S), Deans (W). Hagley Park is inside the avenues on the west; Harper Ave runs along the park's north edge.
- **One-way pairs** (since 1969): Lichfield / St Asaph; Madras (northbound) / Barbadoes (southbound); Montreal (northbound) / Durham (southbound); Salisbury / Kilmore.
- **Cathedral Square** with the intact stone ChristChurch Cathedral at the centre; Colombo St runs N–S through it.
- **City Mall**: Cashel St from Cambridge Tce to High St, plus High St up to Hereford/Colombo; pedestrianised 1982.
- **Bridge of Remembrance**: west end of the mall, Cashel St over the Avon between Oxford and Cambridge Terraces.
- **Arts Centre**: block bounded by Rolleston Ave, Worcester Blvd, Montreal St, Hereford St. Canterbury Museum and the Botanic Gardens are across Rolleston Ave.
- **Heritage tram**: opened 4 Feb 1995. 2.5 km loop: Cathedral Square – Worcester St – Worcester Blvd – Rolleston Ave – Armagh St – New Regent St – back to Worcester St.
- **Town Hall** (1972) on Kilmore St facing **Victoria Square** (Colombo/Armagh/Durham/Kilmore).
- **Antigua Boat Sheds**: built 1882 to 1883 at 2 Cambridge Terrace ([Heritage NZ and others, research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md)). **Punting on the Avon** began commercially in February 1987 from the Edmonds band rotunda, not the Boat Sheds, and was relaunched on 1 April 1989 (search excerpts of The Press only). Where punts left from in 1995, and what the Boat Sheds hired that year, are **(unverified)**: present-day listings name the Boat Sheds and Worcester Bridge, which is not evidence for 1995.
- **Railway station**: moved to Addington on 5 April 1993 ([research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md)). The old Moorhouse Ave station building with its clock tower still stood.
- **Lancaster Park**: Waltham, just south of Moorhouse Ave (Stevens St, Lancaster St, Wilsons Rd). Embankment still standing, no light towers until 1996. "Jade Stadium" naming is later (1998).
- **New Brighton**: no pier in 1995 (old one demolished 1965, new one opened Nov 1997). New Brighton Mall (pedestrian, 1978) was in retail decline after losing its Saturday-trading monopoly.
- **Port Hills**: Dyers Pass Road climbs from Cashmere past the Sign of the Takahe (corner of Hackthorne Road) to the Sign of the Kiwi at the pass, and carries on down to Governors Bay; the Summit Road links the passes along the crest (confirmed, [research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md)). The Sign of the Kiwi was a refreshment place and information centre after a renovation begun in 1989; what the Sign of the Takahe was used as in 1995 is **(unverified)**. [Sugarloaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarloaf_(New_Zealand)) is 493.78 m with a 121 m transmitter tower completed in 1965. The gondola opened in October 1992 as the Mount Cavendish Gondola, from Heathcote Valley.
- **Lyttelton road tunnel**: opened 1964, 1,970 m, from Heathcote to Lyttelton. Lyttelton port and the Timeball Station on the harbour side.
- **SH1**: does NOT run along Riccarton Rd (that is SH73). Leaving the city to the south-west it is Main South Rd through Sockburn, Hornby, Islington, Templeton, Weedons, Rolleston, Burnham, Selwyn, Dunsandel, Bankside to the Rakaia (order confirmed against the Main South Line route and the town pages, [research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md)). SH1 was moved off the city route (Bealey Ave, the Madras and Barbadoes pair, Brougham St, Blenheim Rd, Sockburn, Main South Rd) onto the Harewood, airport and Hornby bypass "in the early 1990s"; the exact year is **(unverified)**, and Sockburn is on the old route only.
- **Boy racers**: Te Ara dates the term "boy racers" to the 1990s ("hoons" before that) and ties it to cheap used Japanese imports ([research file 05](research/05-culture-and-daily-life.md)). Late 1980s reports in The Press of youths and cars on Colombo Street exist as search excerpts only. Friday and Saturday night cruising on Moorhouse Ave and the four avenues in the 1990s specifically is still **(unverified)**: no page that could be opened says so.

## Mid-Canterbury

- **Rakaia Bridge**: 1,756 m, opened 25 March 1939, two narrow lanes (6.2 m), NZ's longest road bridge; separate rail bridge just upstream. Rakaia township is on the south bank.
- **Ashburton**: railway through the centre; SH1 is West St; East St on the other side is the main shopping street; 21 m concrete clock tower (1976) at Baring Square East; river bridge to Tinwald.
- **Rangitata**: SH1 crosses via Rangitata Island (confirmed). The count and lengths of the bridges (two, about 645 m and 356 m) are **(unverified)**.
- **Temuka**: main street is King St. Whether SH1 skirted the centre in 1995 is **(unverified)**: a "Temuka Bypass section of State Highway No. 1" existed in law by 18 May 1995, but a 2025 NZTA bulletin (search excerpt) speaks of "State Highway 1/King Street".
- **Order on SH1** (confirmed, [research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md)): Rakaia, Chertsey (just off the highway), Dromore, Fairton, Ashburton, Tinwald, Winslow, Hinds, Ealing, Rangitata, Orari (along its east edge), Winchester, Temuka, Arowhenua, Opihi River, Washdyke, Smithfield, Timaru.

## Timaru

- **SH1 north to south**: Hilton Highway, Evans St, Theodosia St, Craigie Ave, King St is the present-day sequence. A 1996 Gazette notice (seen as a search excerpt only) appears to have moved SH1 onto Theodosia St and Craigie Ave from Stafford St and King St, so in 1995 SH1 was probably Hilton Highway, Evans St, Stafford St, King St **(unverified, and it contradicts the game's map; see the report)**.
- **Stafford St**: main retail street on the old bullock track; slopes towards the sea; bluestone and brick Victorian–Edwardian frontages; kinks at North St where two surveys meet. Theatre Royal at 118 Stafford St.
- **The Bay Hill**: a real street name; it meets Stafford St above Caroline Bay, and the Hydro Grand Hotel (built 1912, demolished November 2017) stood at number 10 (search excerpts, [research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md)). Whether the Hydro Grand was trading in 1995, and a fire there reported for 19 February 1993, are **(unverified)**.
- **Caroline Bay in 1995**: sound shell (1937 by the council diary, 1938 by a local heritage page), tearooms, aviary, memorial wall; Carnival runs Boxing Day to about 12 January. The Piazza (1997) and rose garden (2001) did not exist yet.
- **Sacred Heart Basilica**: Craigie Ave (on SH1), 1911; twin towers with small domes plus a copper cupola; Oamaru stone and brick.
- **Other landmarks**: St Mary's Anglican (Church St); timber lighthouse (1878) at Māori Park, Te Weka St/Benvenue Ave in 1995; bluestone Landing Service Building (1871) at 2 George St near the station.
- **Port**: artificial harbour from 1877, Port Loop Rd (1972), major fishing port, grain silos. The Main South Line runs through cuttings around the bay with the port on its seaward side.
- **Washdyke**: industrial suburb at the SH8 junction, Phar Lap Raceway, lagoon. **Smithfield freezing works** on Bridge Rd between Caroline Bay and Washdyke was operating in 1995.

## Te reo Māori names

Ōtautahi (Christchurch) · Te Maru (Timaru; Te Tihi-o-Maru is a variant) · Ōtākaro (Avon) · Kā Pākihi-whakatekateka-a-Waitaha (Canterbury Plains) · Kā Tiritiri o te Moana (Southern Alps) · Ōhinehou (Lyttelton) and Whakaraupō (its harbour) · Ngā Kōhatu Whakarakaraka o Tamatea Pōkai Whenua (Port Hills).

Mana whenua (confirmed on [ngaitahu.iwi.nz](https://ngaitahu.iwi.nz/ngai-tahu/papatipu-runanga/arowhenua/) and the rūnanga pages, [research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md)): Ngāi Tūāhuriri, marae at Tuahiwi, from the Hurunui to the Hakatere (Christchurch); Te Hapū o Ngāti Wheke at Rāpaki (Lyttelton and Whakaraupō); Te Rūnanga o Arowhenua, hapū Kāti Huirapa, from the Rakaia to the Waitaki (Temuka and Timaru); Te Rūnanga o Waihao near Waimate. The two takiwā descriptions overlap between the Rakaia and the Hakatere, so Ashburton should not be assigned to one rūnanga alone. Official dual place names date from the 1998 Ngāi Tahu settlement, so 1995 road signage would have been English-only.

Notes on the names above, checked against Kā Huru Manu, the Ngāi Tahu atlas ([kahurumanu.ngaitahu.iwi.nz](https://kahurumanu.ngaitahu.iwi.nz/atlas)), and rūnanga pages; details and quotes in [research file 07](research/07-verification-and-ngai-tahu.md):

- Spellings confirmed as the atlas has them: Ōtautahi, Ōtākaro, Ōpāwaho (Heathcote), Te Ihutai (the estuary), Ōhinehou, Whakaraupō, Aoraki, Rakaia, Hakatere, Ōpihi, Arowhenua, Waitarakao (Washdyke Lagoon), Pātītī Point, Kā Pākihi-whakatekateka-a-Waitaha, Te Umu Kaha (Temuka, three words), Te Heru-o-Kahukura (Sugarloaf), Pukeatua (Dyers Pass), Ōrāri.
- **Timaru**: [RNZ's source](https://www.rnz.co.nz/programmes/nau-mai-town/story/2018889375/nau-mai-town-episode-9-timaru) says Kāti Huirapa prefer Te Maru, with Te Tihi-o-Maru a variant; the atlas headword is plain "Timaru", and Arowhenua's own pages give no Māori name for the town. Which the game should show is an open question for the owner.
- **Kā Tiritiri o te Moana**: the atlas hyphenates it (Kā Tiritiri-o-te-moana); the official dual name is written Kā Tiritiri o Te Moana.
- **Rangitata**: the atlas uses Rangitata; "Rakitata" was not found on a page opened **(unverified)**.
- **Port Hills**: one page prints "Ngā Kohatu Whakarakaraka o Tamatea Pōkai Whenua" and a second spelling, "Whakarekareka", is in circulation; no Ngāi Tahu source or translation was found **(unverified)**.
- **Sumner and New Brighton**: the atlas has no single suburb name. It has Matuku-takotako (Sumner beach), Tuawera (Cave Rock), Ōruapaeroa (New Brighton wetlands) and Te Kōrero Karoro (South Brighton Spit). No Māori name for Caroline Bay was found **(unverified)**.
- **Marae**: Arowhenua marae is on Huirapa Street, about 1 km from Temuka township; its wharenui, Te Hapa o Niu Tireni, opened in 1905, and Arowhenua translate the name as "The Broken Promises of New Zealand" ([ngaitahu.iwi.nz](https://ngaitahu.iwi.nz/ngai-tahu/papatipu-runanga/arowhenua/)). Rehua Marae is on Springfield Road, St Albans, opened 3 December 1960 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehua_marae)). Ngā Hau e Whā opened in May 1990, is described as in Bromley, and is a pan-tribal urban marae, not a rūnanga marae ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81_Hau_e_Wh%C4%81_National_Marae)); its Pages Road address is **(unverified)**. At Rāpaki the carved wharenui Wheke opened in 2008, so it must not appear in 1995.
- **How it was written in 1995**: the Ngāi Tahu magazine Te Karaka of summer 1996 uses no macrons, "Ngai Tahu" far more often than "Kai Tahu", "runanga", "mahinga kai", and "taua" and "poua" for elders, with "Kia kaha" in English prose. The k for ng appears mainly in hapū and place names (Kati Huirapa, Aoraki). The game's subtitles keep modern macrons; this note is for period signage.
- **Needs a Ngāi Tahu reader, not a web page**: whether "Aunty", "e hoa" and "ka pai" sit naturally in South Island Māori English of 1995, and how a hui, kai for a hui and manaakitanga at a marae should be described. No web source settles it **(unverified)**.

## The 1995 fleet: what was on the road

Full tables, links and quotes: [research file 01](research/01-fleet-statistics.md) (statistics), [02](research/02-street-photographs.md) (53 photographs looked at one by one), [03](research/03-working-vehicles.md) (working vehicles and dating details), [08](research/08-vehicle-reference-australian-british.md) and [09](research/09-vehicle-reference-japanese.md) (reference data per model).

- **Fleet by type**, licensed at 31 March 1995 ([NZ Official Yearbook 1996](https://www3.stats.govt.nz/New_Zealand_Official_Yearbooks/1996/NZOYB_1996.html), table 23.9): cars 1,647,134 (plus 13,364 rentals and 4,458 taxis); light trucks, vans and utes 284,327; heavy trucks 71,506; buses and coaches 9,109; motorcycles 54,391; tractors 19,989; trailers 360,311. As shares of powered road vehicles (our arithmetic): cars about 80%, light commercials 13.6%, heavy trucks 3.4%, motorcycles 2.7%, buses 0.4%.
- **Motorcycles had collapsed**: 137,442 licensed in 1985 (1990 Yearbook), 54,391 in 1995.
- **Used Japanese imports** ([MIA figures via RayCee](https://raycee1234.blogspot.com/2014/11/passenger-car-sales-in-nz-new-vs-used.html), matching the Yearbook series): 2,918 of 84,434 car registrations in 1985 (3.5%); 50,966 in 1989 (37.8%); 85,324 in 1990 (53.4%, the first year they passed new cars); 62,088 of 123,853 in 1994 (50.1%); 80,976 of 146,656 in 1995 (55.2%). Import licensing ended on 1 January 1989.
- **Make-up of the 1995 car fleet** (our estimate from those series, not a printed figure): used imports about 22% in March 1995 and about 26% by December; cars sold new in New Zealand from 1985 to 1994 up to about 42%; cars sold new before 1985 at least about 36%.
- **Age**: average car age was 9.93 years in 1992 ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/nz-fleet-ageing-disgracefully/2WFFNU5MKLUHWUHZQVYVDOAQ6U/)) and 11.20 in 1998 ([Infometrics](https://economics.infometrics.co.nz/article/2009-08-road-trip-down-memory-lane)). No 1995 figure was found; about 10 to 11 years is our interpolation **(unverified)**. Used imports averaged 6.07 years old on arrival in 1991.
- **New-car brands, 1985 to 1994 summed** (MIA via RayCee, our arithmetic): Ford 19%, Toyota 18%, Mitsubishi 14%, Holden 11%, Nissan 9%, Honda 9%, Mazda 6%.
- **Best sellers**: Holden Kingswood and Belmont, over 10,000 in [1973](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1974/01/new-zealand-1973-holden-kingswoodbelmont-hits-record-heights/); Ford Cortina from [1977 to 1983](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1984/01/new-zealand-1977-1983-ford-cortina-best-seller/), over 100,000 assembled; Toyota Corolla [1984 to 1986](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1987/01/new-zealand-1984-1986-toyota-corolla-reigns/); Holden Commodore 9,888 in [1987](https://bestsellingcarsblog.com/1988/01/new-zealand-1987-holden-commodore-reclaims-lead-off-corolla/), with Corolla, Falcon, Mirage, Laser and Sigma behind it. The best-selling model for each year from 1988 to 1995 is **(unverified)**; only the brand leader is known (Ford in 1988, Toyota from 1989). Sources disagree on 1982 to 1984 (Cortina, Corolla or Laser).
- **The older strands**: in the late 1960s British cars were "more than half of all new cars" and Australian brands "another third" ([Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/cars-and-the-motor-industry/page-2)); in 1975 British Leyland was still the largest brand (16,215 of 79,701); Japan passed the UK as the main source in 1978. 1972 to 1974 were the biggest registration years before 1989. Local assembly end dates: Morris Minor 1974, Valiant and Hillman Hunter and Triumph 2000/2500 1979, Hillman Avenger and Ford Escort 1980, Morris Marina and Vauxhall Chevette 1981, Ford Cortina June 1983 ([research file 08](research/08-vehicle-reference-australian-british.md)).
- **Christchurch built cars**: Steel Motor Assemblies in Addington assembled from 1964, built the first New Zealand Toyota (a Corona, February 1967 by Wikipedia, 1966 by Driven), became Toyota New Zealand Christchurch and closed in September 1996, so it was working in 1995 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_New_Zealand), [Driven](https://www.drivencarguide.co.nz/news/toyota-celebrates-60-years-in-new-zealand/)). From 1990 all Holdens were imported built-up; the last locally assembled Falcon was the EB II.
- **Names that differ here**: the NZ "Sentra" of 1986 to 1998 was the Nissan Pulsar; "Sigma" was the Todd-assembled Mitsubishi Galant (rear-drive, then front-drive from mid-1984); the Mitsubishi V3000 (1988 to 1996) was a New Zealand-only name for a locally built 3.0 V6 Galant, chosen by the traffic patrol to replace the turbo Sigma GSR ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Galant)); the third-generation Subaru Leone was sold as the "Omega". Ladas came through the Dairy Board "in lieu of cash payments for deliveries of mutton and butter to the Soviet Union", the last such trade in 1990 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada_Riva)).
- **The mark of a used import**: guard-mounted mirrors, Japanese dealer decals and higher specification ([Christian Science Monitor, June 1992, in research file 01](research/01-fleet-statistics.md); the Wikipedia Bluebird page says the mirrors "distinguished thousands of used cars imported from Japan").
- **What the photographs show** ([research file 02](research/02-street-photographs.md); class-level judgements from small images): on Stafford Street about 1990, of roughly 110 vehicles, about 52% were 1980s Japanese small and mid-size sedans and hatchbacks, 10% big Australian sedans and wagons (mostly 1970s), 10% vans (nearly all white Japanese forward-control), 9% British and European survivors, 8% plain white Japanese utes, 5% 1970s Japanese cars. White dominates; mustard, orange, bright green and pale blue belong to the 1970s cars; nearly everything is on steel wheels with hubcaps. Timaru shows more 1970s Australian and British cars and more utes on the main street than Christchurch, which shows newer Japanese cars, courier vans and a few Europeans (a Saab 900, a BMW, a Lada Niva on [Moorhouse Ave in 1996](https://discoverywall.nz/media/59551)). **Limits**: no firmly dated 1995 photograph of ordinary kerbside parking was found for either town, nothing for Ashburton, Temuka or SH1, and the Timaru set is about five years older than the Christchurch one.
- **Boy-racer cars of 1993 to 1996**: no source gives a list **(unverified)**. Enthusiast accounts name the Familia DOHC turbo, early Skyline turbos, a Sprinter twin cam and the CR-X ([research file 09](research/09-vehicle-reference-japanese.md)).
- **Canterbury-specific fleet figures** (counts, age, ute share): none found **(unverified)**.

### Working vehicles

- **Police, 1995**: all-white Holden Commodore VR sedan (and wagon in a [Timaru photograph of 12 August 1995](https://timdc.pastperfectonline.com/photo/66A03895-031B-4BFD-BB06-788787085650)). A band of slanted blue dashes runs nose to tail at door-handle height; the front door carries a solid blue panel with a thin red line on its leading edge and white italic "POLICE"; "POLICE" in blue with a red slash on the bonnet; roof bar red on the driver's half and blue on the other with a siren speaker between; steel wheels; no door crest ([111emergency.co.nz](https://www.111emergency.co.nz/N-R/Police-Vintage4.htm), photograph of the 1994 car TI906, looked at by us).
- **Ex-traffic cars**: the Ministry of Transport's Traffic Safety Service merged into Police on 1 July 1992. Its cars (Mitsubishi V3000 1988 to 1992, Ford Falcon) were black with white roof and doors; after the merger they wore the Police door graphics on that scheme ([111emergency.co.nz](https://www.111emergency.co.nz/J-M/MOT2.htm), [1 News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/02/12/the-eighties-calling-police-roll-out-restored-1988-patrol-car)). A 1994 unmarked speed camera car was a plain Ford Telstar wagon parked on a verge with the tailgate up.
- **Not 1995**: blue and yellow checkered Highway Patrol livery ([February 2001](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/GE0102/S00028/new-look-police-patrol-cars-more-visible.htm)); the Highway Patrol itself (launched 15 December 2000, though Te Ara says 1992).
- **Christchurch buses**: 69 MAN SL202 (1986 to 1990) did the regular work and Bristol RELL "Hess" buses (1978 to 1981) the peak extras; livery "red with a white, central horizontal stripe", branded Big Red Bus by August 1992 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch_Transport_Board), [Showbus photograph](https://showbus.co.uk/gallery/foreign/nz/redbusman.htm)). Canride buses were mustard-yellow and white in 1991 photographs. A private operator ran second-hand ex-municipal buses on some routes until January 1997.
- **Timaru buses**: the council service passed to a private operator in 1991 ([Omnibus Society](https://www.omnibus.org.nz/history/opert.html)); photographs show white with blue bands about 1990 and a red and white MAN in 1995. Models are **(unverified)**.
- **On SH1**: four coach services each way between Christchurch and Timaru (white with a green skirt and purple stripe in an October 1995 timetable), plus shuttles; the Southerner train left Christchurch at 8.15 am and came back through Timaru at 3.13 pm ([talkingtransport.com](https://talkingtransport.com/2024/03/04/long-distance-passenger-transport/)). Heavy vehicles and buses were limited to 90 km/h and anything towing to 80 (Yearbook 1996).
- **Taxis**: a 1990 Christchurch City Libraries photograph shows a white big Ford sedan with a door decal and roof bar ([research file 03](research/03-working-vehicles.md)). Typical 1995 models and other firms' liveries are **(unverified)**.
- **Trucks on the plains**: International ACCO, Volvo FL10 grain trucks and Hino trucks with three-deck stock crates and matching trailers ([NZ Trucking](https://nztrucking.co.nz/?p=132897)). Bedford TK trucks were assembled at Petone ([research file 08](research/08-vehicle-reference-australian-british.md)). Crate construction in the 1990s is **(unverified)**.
- **Farm bikes and tractors**: three-wheelers stopped being made in 1988, so a 1995 farm ATV is a quad ([Rural News](https://www.ruralnewsgroup.co.nz/dairy-news/dairy-machinery-products/changing-face-of-farm-transport)); the Honda CT110 was the postie bike. Which farm bikes, tractors and farm utes were commonest in Canterbury is **(unverified)**: the sites that would say refused automated reading.

### The details that date a car

- **Plates** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_New_Zealand)): two letters and one to four digits until 2001; black on reflective white from November 1986 (MX100 the first), silver on black before that and still valid; slashed zero from mid-1990. First series by year: 1987 NA, 1988 NO, 1989 OC, 1990 OR, 1991 PM, 1992 RE, 1993 RS, 1994 SG, 1995 TB, 1996 TU. No Q and no V as a first letter.
- **Warrant and licence** (Yearbooks 1995 and 1996): warrant of fitness every six months, yearly for vehicles under six years old first registered after 1 August 1987, costing $20.62; "a new-style label introduced in 1995", with a barcode, shows the licence inside the windscreen. What the stickers looked like, and where they sat, is **(unverified)**.
- **Fuel**: grades were "super" and "regular", leaded still sold; 1995 averages 95.0 and 90.0 cents a litre ([figure.nz, MBIE data](https://figure.nz/chart/YPxIJBkBedBDR1hE-yk75dBd3IV9SJ1e9)).
- **Accessories, wear and paint charts** (tow bars, roof racks, seat covers, dog boxes, bull bars, mags, rust): not researched beyond what the photographs show **(unverified)**.

## The era: Canterbury and South Canterbury, 1990 to 1996

Full notes: [research file 04](research/04-era-1990-1996.md). "Y95" and "Y96" are the [1995](https://www3.stats.govt.nz/New_Zealand_Official_Yearbooks/1995/NZOYB_1995.html) and [1996](https://www3.stats.govt.nz/New_Zealand_Official_Yearbooks/1996/NZOYB_1996.html) Official Yearbooks, whose chronology and weather sections are the best single source found.

- **The works**: Fortex Group (Seafield near Ashburton, Silverstream near Mosgiel) went into receivership on 23 March 1994, "with the loss of 2,000 jobs in Tauranga, Ashburton and Mosgiel; farmers are owed $7.5 million" (Y95, checked by us; [Brian Easton](https://www.eastonbh.ac.nz/1996/05/exfortex/) says 1,800). Islington was a closed works from 1988 ([ODT](https://www.odt.co.nz/business/ppcs-consigned-history)); Kaiapoi closed in 1991 ([Waimakariri District Council](https://landmarks.waimakariri.govt.nz/kaiapoi-heritage)). Smithfield was operating in 1995 as an Alliance plant ([South Canterbury Museum](https://timdc.pastperfectonline.com/archive/2D8D3544-6E67-40D4-8ACA-467875391240)). **Layoffs at Smithfield in 1994 to 1996 were not found (unverified)**; the story therefore opens with a seasonal man not called back for the new season, which needs no documented layoff, rather than the mass layoff it originally claimed.
- **Not plot material**: the Fortex fraud convictions (March 1996), and the tragedies and criminal cases of 1994 to 1996 listed in research file 04. The collapse and its effect on the district are usable; the people are not.
- **Money**: unemployment 128,100, or 7.5%, in the year to March 1995, down from 172,600 in 1992; the Māori rate 19.4% (Y96). State house rents rose 12.4% in the year to June 1995 under market rents (Y96). Mortgage rates rose from 7.4% in early 1994 to about 10.4% by early 1996. June quarter 1995 prices (Y96): meat pie $2.00, hamburger $2.41, white sliced loaf $1.52, 2 litres of milk $2.37, newspaper 58c, stamp 45c. Regional unemployment, the price of a jug and the 1995 minimum wage are **(unverified)**.
- **Rail and port**: New Zealand Rail, sold on 30 September 1993, was renamed Tranz Rail on 18 October 1995 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranz_Rail)). Lyttelton Port Company was council owned and listed in 1996. Nothing dated was found on Timaru's port in 1995 **(unverified)**.
- **1994**: 17 to 21 March, South Canterbury flood, the Opihi at about 3,300 cumecs, SH1 closed at Pareora and Otaio ([NIWA](https://hwe.niwa.co.nz/event/March_1994_Canterbury_and_Otago_Flooding)); 3 September, Canterbury took the Ranfurly Shield from Waikato 29-26 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranfurly_Shield_1990%E2%80%931999)); November, the Christchurch Casino opened, the country's first (Y95; the day, 3 or 4 November, is **unverified**); December, the $1 billion "fiscal envelope".
- **1995, in order**: 4 February, the tram returns and a Test against the West Indies is on at Lancaster Park; 26 February, 37.0C at Geraldine, the year's national high (Y96); 5 May, London Street in Lyttelton cordoned off for a film shoot; 13 May (San Diego), the America's Cup won 5-0 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_America%27s_Cup)), with the red socks ([Sir Peter Blake Trust](https://blakenz.org/?p=3243)); 13 June, France announces eight tests at Mururoa, HMNZS Tui sails 19 August, first test 5 September ([Navy Museum](https://navymuseum.co.nz/explore/by-themes/1970-today/operation-valerian/)); 24 June, the World Cup final lost 15-12, and the same day Canterbury beat Mid Canterbury 64-19 in a Shield match at Ashburton; 29 July, Canterbury beat South Canterbury 72-17; 26 August, rugby union declared open; 7 September, Christchurch's first cyber-café; 18 and 23 September, Ruapehu erupts; 23 September, Canterbury lose the Shield 0-35 to Auckland; 18 October, Tranz Rail; Friday 10 November, Show Day (Y95: "Canterbury observes its anniversary day on the Friday of Show Week"); 11 to 13 December, torrential rain, the Rangitata over its banks (Y96).
- **Winter 1995**: the heaviest South Island snowfalls in 20 years; in September snow inland in South Canterbury (45 cm at Tekapo) with about 50,000 lambs lost and then a further 50,000 (Y96).
- **Ngāi Tahu claim**: negotiations begun in 1991 were "suspended unilaterally by the Crown in 1994" and resumed in 1996; Heads of Agreement 5 October 1996, Deed of Settlement 21 November 1997, Act 29 September 1998 ([Te Rūnanga o Ngāi Tahu](https://ngaitahu.iwi.nz/ngai-tahu/creation-stories/the-settlement/claim-history/)). So the claim was stalled all through 1995.
- **Local detail not found** **(unverified)**: Caroline Bay Carnival specifics, Phar Lap Raceway, Washdyke and Ashburton saleyards and stock firms, the Addington saleyards' status, dated nor'west wind damage, a Christchurch anti-nuclear march of 14 July 1995 (search summary only). Papers Past holds The Press to the end of 1995 but refuses automated reading; these are worth a manual look.

## Daily life in 1995

Full notes: [research file 05](research/05-culture-and-daily-life.md).

- **The dial, Christchurch** ([Wikipedia station pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newstalk_ZB), in research file 05): talkback on 1098 AM (3ZB until 1994); its music on a "classic hits" FM; 91ZM; C93FM, classic rock, which had absorbed Radio Avon in 1990; an easy-listening station from 1994; More FM from 1991; Radio Pacific talk and racing on 93.7; student radio RDU on 98.3; Plains FM access radio on 96.9 since 1988; National Radio on 675 AM; Tahu FM on 90.5 from 6 February 1991. The Edge, The Rock and Channel Z arrived in 1999, Radio Sport in 1996.
- **The dial, Timaru and Ashburton**: [Radio Caroline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Caroline_(New_Zealand)) (3XC 1949, 1152 AM) went to FM in 1995; a private local station, [Port FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_FM), began in 1995; Ashburton's 3ZE went to FM in 1995.
- **Stafford Street about 1990** (South Canterbury Museum captions and signs read from photographs): a variety chain, a bargain shop in a small mall, a building-society bank, family-name chemists, a cake shop, an appliance court, a hotel, plain "Takeaways" and "Fish & Chips", and a sign banning stock trucks from the main street.
- **Shops and banks**: McDonald's reached Christchurch on [3 November 1987](https://www.newstalkzb.co.nz/news/business/the-first-mcdonalds-in-christchurch-opened-30-years-ago-today); D.I.C. was gone by 1991; Farmers and DEKA were one company from 1992; Trust Bank (1986 to 1996) had absorbed the Canterbury and South Canterbury savings banks. Fast food in Timaru, supermarket banners and petrol brands are **(unverified)**.
- **Drink**: purchase age 20; wine but not beer in supermarkets; no Sunday bottle sales; smoking legal in bars. Canterbury Draught was Ward's Beer rebranded in 1990 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_(Australasian_company))); the Timaru brewery was the Mainland Brewery ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_Breweries)). That Timaru drank its beer is our inference **(unverified)**.
- **Gambling**: Lotto on Saturday nights only, Instant Kiwi from 1989, the TAB taking racing bets only until 1996, pub gaming machines from 1988, the casino from November 1994.
- **Night**: a 1995 rave filled the old West End theatre in Cathedral Square with about 1,200 people; raves in the Port Hills ([Cityscape](https://cityscape-christchurch.co.nz/blog/entry/a-toast-to-the-rave-days)). NZ number ones of 1994 included "Hip Hop Holiday" and Supergroove; no NZ act had a new number one in 1995.
- **Phones and money**: 03 and seven-digit numbers arrived between 1989 and 1993 (South Canterbury had been 056); mobiles were 025; rural party lines rang in Morse patterns ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_numbers_in_New_Zealand)). 1c and 2c coins went in 1990, $1 and $2 coins came in 1991; home-delivered glass milk bottles lasted in Christchurch until 2005.
- **Words**: "boy racers" in the 1990s, "hoons" before; "crib" in Otago and Southland, "bach" further north; "bogan" generally, "Westie" in Auckland (Te Ara).
- **Thin** **(unverified)**: food and drink brands, how far north cheese rolls went, clothes beyond the Swanndri, most slang, TV advertising, small-town shop lists.

## Crime and policing as they were

Full notes: [research file 06](research/06-crime-and-policing.md).

- **Unarmed**: "Police in New Zealand have generally been unarmed" ([Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/police)). The Armed Offenders Squad (1964) are part-timers who are paged, "assemble at their base, to draw arms", then "cordon, contain and appeal" ([NZ Police](https://www.police.govt.nz/about-us/structure/police-teams-and-units/armed-offenders-squads)); shots are fired about once in 260 deployments ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Offenders_Squad)). A lock box with firearms in every frontline car dates from 2012; in 2010 only 641 of about 2,700 vehicles had gun safes ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/hundreds-of-police-cars-routinely-carry-arms/23B4JZQIYV7KD742EHXCFT4FOI/)). **This contradicts the game, whose police shoot from three lights; see the report.**
- **Had in 1995**: the PR-24 baton, dogs, the white beat helmet in its last year ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/police-beanies-get-ok-helmets-must-wait/CKTDQDAFAN2KPHHBWQTJ22GLAQ/)). **Did not have**: Tasers, stab vests, a Christchurch helicopter. The service pistol and its adoption date, pepper spray's date and body armour are **(unverified)**.
- **Traffic**: speed cameras and compulsory breath testing with "booze buses" both began in 1993 (Te Ara); the 1995 road toll was 582. The street-racing Act is 2003 and the car-crushing law 2009; neither belongs in the game.
- **Rackets, by kind**: black-market crayfish and paua sold for cash, bypassing the licensed fish receiver, with vehicles and vessels liable to seizure ([MAF](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PO1203/S00230/fishing-vessel-seized-in-canterbury.htm), a later Canterbury example of the type); car conversion, stealing to order and "chop shops" ([Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/dishonesty-crime/print)); wound-back odometers on used imports, about 30% by a Customs inspector's estimate ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/clocked-imports-running-at-30pc/RI4VX7JLQXSLAZIVPAYHJVNIKM/)); cannabis growing and tinnie houses, with methamphetamine not a factor until the late 1990s; "taxing", defined by [Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/gangs/print) as "extorting money for a perceived offence or for performance of a service such as protection"; stock theft by truck, dogs and yards at night ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/top/474168/rustling-in-the-spotlight-after-hundreds-of-sheep-stolen-from-north-canterbury-farm), a modern source for the method).
- **Customary fishing** in 1995 ran under regulation 27 of the Fisheries (Amateur Fishing) Regulations 1986: written authorisation from a person appointed by tangata whenua, for a hui or tangi ([government release](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA0404/S00110.htm)). It is lawful and the story treats it so.
- **Banned names**: research file 06 lists the real gangs of the period so that the invented crew resembles none of them.
- **Not found** **(unverified)**: waterfront pilfering after 1989, illegal bookmakers, loan sharks, meat theft from the works, 1995 pursuit policy, rural and sole-charge station practice, anything specific to Timaru.
