# Research notes: Christchurch, Timaru and SH1, c. 1995

Compiled from English Wikipedia, Christchurch City Libraries' chronology, WuHoo Timaru and present-day OpenStreetMap street names. Items marked **(unverified)** come from general knowledge and could not be sourced; treat them as plausible rather than certain.

## Christchurch (pre-earthquake)

- **Four avenues**: Bealey (N), Fitzgerald (E), Moorhouse (S), Deans (W). Hagley Park is inside the avenues on the west; Harper Ave runs along the park's north edge.
- **One-way pairs** (since 1969): Lichfield / St Asaph; Madras (northbound) / Barbadoes (southbound); Montreal (northbound) / Durham (southbound); Salisbury / Kilmore.
- **Cathedral Square** with the intact stone ChristChurch Cathedral at the centre; Colombo St runs N–S through it.
- **City Mall**: Cashel St from Cambridge Tce to High St, plus High St up to Hereford/Colombo; pedestrianised 1982.
- **Bridge of Remembrance**: west end of the mall, Cashel St over the Avon between Oxford and Cambridge Terraces.
- **Arts Centre**: block bounded by Rolleston Ave, Worcester Blvd, Montreal St, Hereford St. Canterbury Museum and the Botanic Gardens are across Rolleston Ave.
- **Heritage tram**: opened 4 Feb 1995. 2.5 km loop: Cathedral Square – Worcester St – Worcester Blvd – Rolleston Ave – Armagh St – New Regent St – back to Worcester St.
- **Town Hall** (1972) on Kilmore St facing **Victoria Square** (Colombo/Armagh/Durham/Kilmore).
- **Punting on the Avon**: Antigua Boat Sheds (Cambridge Tce) and Worcester Bridge. Mid-90s departure points **(unverified)**.
- **Railway station**: moved to Addington in April 1993. The old Moorhouse Ave station building with its clock tower still stood.
- **Lancaster Park**: Waltham, just south of Moorhouse Ave (Stevens St, Lancaster St, Wilsons Rd). Embankment still standing, no light towers until 1996. "Jade Stadium" naming is later (1998).
- **New Brighton**: no pier in 1995 (old one demolished 1965, new one opened Nov 1997). New Brighton Mall (pedestrian, 1978) was in retail decline after losing its Saturday-trading monopoly.
- **Port Hills**: Dyers Pass Rd climbs from Cashmere past the Sign of the Takahe to the Sign of the Kiwi at the pass **(road name unverified)**; Summit Road runs along the crest; Sugarloaf (494 m) carries the TV tower; the Gondola (1992) runs from Heathcote Valley to Mt Cavendish.
- **Lyttelton road tunnel**: opened 1964, 1,970 m, from Heathcote to Lyttelton. Lyttelton port and the Timeball Station on the harbour side.
- **SH1**: does NOT run along Riccarton Rd (that is SH73). Leaving the city to the south-west it is Main South Rd through Sockburn, Hornby, Islington, Templeton, Weedons, Rolleston, Burnham, Dunsandel, Bankside to the Rakaia **(order unverified)**.
- **Boy racers**: Friday/Saturday-night cruising on Colombo St, Moorhouse Ave and the four avenues **(unverified recollection)**.

## Mid-Canterbury

- **Rakaia Bridge**: 1,756 m, 1939, two narrow lanes (6.2 m), NZ's longest road bridge; separate rail bridge just upstream. Rakaia township is on the south bank.
- **Ashburton**: railway through the centre; SH1 is West St; East St on the other side is the main shopping street; 21 m concrete clock tower (1976) at Baring Square East; river bridge to Tinwald.
- **Rangitata**: SH1 crosses via Rangitata Island on two bridges (≈645 m and ≈356 m).
- **Temuka**: main street is King St; SH1 skirts the centre.
- **Order on SH1** **(unverified)**: Rakaia – Chertsey – Ashburton – Tinwald – Hinds – Rangitata – Orari – Winchester – Temuka – Arowhenua – Opihi River – Washdyke – Timaru.

## Timaru

- **SH1 north to south**: Hilton Highway – Evans St – Theodosia St – Craigie Ave – King St.
- **Stafford St**: main retail street on the old bullock track; slopes towards the sea; bluestone and brick Victorian–Edwardian frontages; kinks at North St where two surveys meet. Theatre Royal at 118 Stafford St.
- **The Bay Hill**: street above Caroline Bay at the north end of Stafford St **(position unverified)**.
- **Caroline Bay in 1995**: sound shell (1937/38), tearooms, aviary, memorial wall; Carnival runs Boxing Day to about 12 January. The Piazza (1997) and rose garden (2001) did not exist yet.
- **Sacred Heart Basilica**: Craigie Ave (on SH1), 1911; twin towers with small domes plus a copper cupola; Oamaru stone and brick.
- **Other landmarks**: St Mary's Anglican (Church St); timber lighthouse (1878) at Māori Park, Te Weka St/Benvenue Ave in 1995; bluestone Landing Service Building (1871) at 2 George St near the station.
- **Port**: artificial harbour from 1877, Port Loop Rd (1972), major fishing port, grain silos. The Main South Line runs through cuttings around the bay with the port on its seaward side.
- **Washdyke**: industrial suburb at the SH8 junction, Phar Lap Raceway, lagoon. **Smithfield freezing works** on Bridge Rd between Caroline Bay and Washdyke was operating in 1995.

## Te reo Māori names

Ōtautahi (Christchurch) · Te Tihi-o-Maru (Timaru) · Ōtākaro (Avon) · Kā Pākihi-whakatekateka-a-Waitaha (Canterbury Plains) · Kā Tiritiri o te Moana (Southern Alps) · Ōhinehou (Lyttelton) and Whakaraupō (its harbour) · Ngā Kōhatu Whakarakaraka o Tamatea Pōkai Whenua (Port Hills).

Mana whenua: Te Rūnanga o Arowhenua (Timaru district); Ngāi Tūāhuriri (Christchurch) **(partly unverified)**. Official dual place names date from the 1998 Ngāi Tahu settlement, so 1995 road signage would have been English-only.
