# The road south: add-ons to come

Status: PLANNED (November 2026 web research pass); nothing here is built.

03 is the whole South Island's phone code, so the title covers everything beyond Timaru. Three add-ons, in order: Oamaru, Dunedin, the inland lakes. Convention as in `RESEARCH.md`: every factual item carries a source link or is marked **(unverified)**.

## 1. Oamaru

**Names and takiwā.** The atlas headword is Ōamaru, a kāinga nohoanga and mahinga kai ([Kā Huru Manu](https://kahurumanu.ngaitahu.iwi.nz/atlas)); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oamaru)'s "Te Oha-a-Maru" is not in the atlas, so use Ōamaru. The atlas also has Makotukutuku (Cape Wanbrow) and Te Kaihīnaki (the Moeraki Boulders). North of the Waitaki the takiwā are Arowhenua's and Waihao's; south is Te Rūnanga o Moeraki, "from Waitaki to Waihemo and inland to the Main Divide" ([Te Manahuna Aoraki](https://www.temanahunaaoraki.org/partner/manawhenua/)), marae at Tenby Street, Moeraki ([ngaitahu.iwi.nz](https://ngaitahu.iwi.nz/ngai-tahu/papatipu-runanga/moeraki/)).

**There in 1995.**
- SH1 through town; shops on Thames and Severn Streets; the 1907 Opera House ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oamaru)).
- The whitestone precinct on Harbour and Tyne Streets: Whitestone Civic Trust 1987 (Wikipedia), heritage festival annual from 1987 ([ODT](https://www.odt.co.nz/regions/north-otago/heritage-aspects-drove-festival-creator), [Oamaru Mail](https://oamarumail.co.nz/news/passion-for-heritage-still-burns)); half restored, half wool stores in 1995: our reading **(unverified)**.
- The harbour: breakwater 1884; since the last coaster in 1974, "a handful of inshore fishing vessels and recreational craft" ([Heritage NZ](https://www.heritage.org.nz/list-details/7536/Listing)).
- Blue penguins settled the harbour quarry in the early 1990s, volunteers from 1992 (Wikipedia); the 1995 visitor set-up **(unverified)**.
- Pukeuri freezing works (1914) at the SH83 junction (Wikipedia); its 1995 owner **(unverified)**.
- The SH1 Waitaki bridge at Glenavy (details **unverified**); Moeraki, 2.5 km off SH1, hurt by "poor catches of rock lobster in the 1990s" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moeraki)); the Southerner stopped at Oamaru ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southerner_(New_Zealand_train))).

**Vehicles.** Nothing new: Hino-class stock trucks to Pukeuri, white utes, the 1970s strand thicker on a poorer coast; plus one prop, a fishing boat on a trailer (360,311 trailers licensed 1995, Yearbook via `RESEARCH.md`) behind a Kingswood (1971 to 1980) or Falcon XF wagon.

**Story hooks.** A salvage racket stripping carved stone and iron from the empty precinct stores; a Moeraki crew running black-market crayfish past the licensed receiver (`RESEARCH.md`), set against lawful customary gathering for a hui at the marae, treated with respect; stolen stock to a buyer at the works' back gate; a night watch against egg thieves at the penguin quarry.

**Map and streaming.** 80 km from Timaru ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oamaru)) at the game's ~1:17 compression (`DESIGN.md` section 2) is ~4.7 km of road plus ~1.6 km of town: extend the world south ~6.5 km, 26 rows of 256 m chunks (to 6.1 × 18.8 km), joined where King Street ends. Contiguous: a braided Waitaki bridge (Rakaia pattern), an SH83 stub, the Thames Street grid, the Tyne/Harbour precinct, mole and quarry under Cape Wanbrow, Moeraki off-highway. No new terrain primitives. New `CityId` `oamaru`; district types suffice; landmark kinds `opera-house`, `whitestone-store`, `penguin-quarry`; `smithfield-works` reused at Pukeuri. No new performance risk.

## 2. Dunedin

**Names and takiwā.** Ngāi Tahu writes the city Ōtepoti ([ngaitahu.iwi.nz](https://ngaitahu.iwi.nz/connect-2/connect/news-and-stories/kai-tahu-designs-incorporated-in-otepoti-central-city-upgrade/)); its translation varies by source **(unverified)**. Otago is from Ōtākou, the kāinga at the harbour mouth, where the Treaty was signed in 1840 ([ngaitahu.iwi.nz](https://ngaitahu.iwi.nz/ngai-tahu/papatipu-runanga/otakou/)). Two takiwā meet here ([Te Kāhui Māngai](https://tkm.govt.nz/regionalauthority/otago-regional-council/)): Kāti Huirapa Rūnaka ki Puketeraki, marae at Karitāne ([puketeraki.nz](https://www.puketeraki.nz/)), Waihemo to Purehurehu; Te Rūnanga o Ōtākou on the peninsula, Purehurehu to Te Mata-au (the Clutha).

**There in 1995.**
- The Octagon: Municipal Chambers, St Paul's, the Regent, the Burns statue; George and Princes Streets bisect it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Octagon,_Dunedin)).
- University of Otago (1869, the country's oldest), the Clocktower complex on the Leith ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Otago)); a 1993 fee-protest clash there, a 1996 occupation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarfie)). Student flats north of campus; a 1990 Castle Street riot burnt a sofa ([ODT](https://odt.co.nz/node/183024)), but when couch burning became the ritual is **(unverified)** (legal until 2013, [Critic](https://www.critic.co.nz/features/article/7863/the-eternal-flame-of-couch-burning)).
- Carisbrook, Caversham: "the House of Pain", terraces, floodlit in the 1990s, SH1 on its north fence, Hillside workshops next door ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carisbrook)).
- The 1906 Flemish-renaissance railway station, city-bought 1994 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin_railway_station)); the brewery on Rattray Street since 1876 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speight%27s); an invented southern brand in game).
- Port Chalmers, container terminal since 1971; 1993 expansion cut part of Observation Point over protest ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Chalmers)). Taiaroa Head's albatross centre open since 1989 ([albatross.org.nz](https://albatross.org.nz/about/)).
- Baldwin Street, Guinness-steepest since 1987, the Gutbuster run since 1988 (the Jaffa race is 2002) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_Street)). SH1 arrives over the Kilmog: a 300 m climb in 5 km, crawler lanes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilmog)).
- Dunedin sound: heyday mid-to-late 80s, still recording in 1995; its rooms the Empire Hotel and Oriental Tavern ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin_sound)); the game invents a fading band and venue.

**Vehicles.** The student bomb is the roster's 1970s strand (Datsun 120Y 1973 to 1978, Cortina Mk IV/V 1976 to 1983, Mini, Valiant) rusted a grade further; what students actually drove is **(unverified)**. A Bedford TK as the invented brewery's dray. Nothing new required.

**Story hooks.** Recovering an invented band's stolen gear; scalping outside Carisbrook; a wages job at the invented brewery; container pilfering at Port Chalmers; a dare run up Baldwin Street; a hijack on the Kilmog crawler lane. Couch fires stay ambient set-dressing, never missions, while their start date is unverified. Nothing touches the Aramoana shootings or the Bain case in any form.

**Map and streaming.** Oamaru to Dunedin is "120 km" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oamaru)): ~7 km compressed plus ~3 km of city and harbour, so ~10 km more south, 39 chunk rows, world ~6.1 × 28.8 km. The first hill city: harbour as an inlet, ridges for the hills. Risks against `DESIGN.md` section 5: authored grades stay near 1:4 so the raycast cars keep traction, Baldwin Street sold visually at a capped grade; downhill sightlines show many chunks, so density stays low south of the Octagon; the 32 m far mesh roughly triples its vertices, so tile or coarsen it beyond ~8 km. New `CityId` `dunedin`, `portchalmers`; a possible `campus` district type; landmark kinds `octagon`, `dunedin-station`, `clocktower`, `carisbrook`, `brewery`, `baldwin-street`, `albatross-head`.

## 3. The inland lakes

**Names and takiwā.** "Tekapo" misspells Takapō ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tekapo)); Ngāi Tahu's Dark Sky Project writes Takapō ([darkskyproject.co.nz](https://darkskyproject.co.nz/our-story/takapo/)). LINZ's Māori oral history atlas prints Takapō, Pūkāki and Ō Hau ([LINZ](https://www.linz.govt.nz/our-work/new-zealand-geographic-board/place-name-stories/maori-oral-history-atlas/rakaihautu-naming-great-lakes-canoe-aoraki)); common print has Pūkaki and Ōhau, so check Kā Huru Manu before the names ship **(spelling unverified)**. Aoraki is atlas-confirmed (`RESEARCH.md`). The basin, Te Manahuna, is shared ground of Arowhenua, Waihao and Moeraki ([Te Manahuna Aoraki](https://www.temanahunaaoraki.org/partner/manawhenua/)): credit all three rūnanga.

**There in 1995.**
- The hydro scheme is finished: Tekapo A (1951), the 26 km canal to Tekapo B on Pūkaki from the 1970s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tekapo)); construction ended 1986 and the stations sat with ECNZ until the 1996/1999 splits ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_Corporation_of_New_Zealand)); the game's operator is invented.
- Twizel: built 1968 by the Ministry of Works, meant to be demolished, saved by its residents and handed to the county by 1984; population 1,017 in 1991 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twizel)).
- Takapō township: Church of the Good Shepherd (1935), Mount John observatory above (date **unverified**) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tekapo)). Tekapo Military Camp hosted US winter exercises by 1984 ([Papers Past](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/CHP19840725.2.82)), still trains the army ([NZDF](https://www.nzdf.mil.nz/where-we-are/tekapo/)); founding date **(unverified)**.
- Salmon farms in the canals "since the 1990s" ([Meridian](https://www.meridianenergy.co.nz/news-and-events/salmon-farms-team-up-to-go-electric)); there in 1995 specifically **(unverified)**.
- Rabbit plagues through the late 1980s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbits_in_New_Zealand)); the RCD import application was declined July 1997, the virus illegally released near Cromwell weeks later ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_haemorrhagic_disease)). In 1995 the push is live and legal: rumour only.
- Merino stations and tour coaches; Fairlie and Burkes Pass the gateway, 57 and 83 km from Washdyke on SH8 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_State_Highway_8)).
- Aoraki is Ngāi Tahu's most sacred ancestor; climbing it is held inappropriate ([DOC](https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-go/canterbury/places/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/historic-aoraki-mount-cook/)). Dual naming came with the 1998 settlement (`RESEARCH.md`): 1995 signs read "Mt Cook".

**Vehicles.** Hiluxes (1983 to 1988) and Land Rover Series IIIs in an invented power-board livery; farm bikes. One new model, a high-floor tour coach; which coaches NZ fleets ran is **(unverified)**, as are the army trucks at the camp.

**Story hooks.** Copper lifted from the invented operator's half-mothballed Twizel depot; salmon rustled from a canal cage at night; a chilly bin over Burkes Pass, no questions asked: the rabbit-cure rumour stays a rumour (the real 1997 release was a real crime); wool theft from an invented station; a race on the dead-straight canal road. Mount Cook village is a scenic terminus protected like the marae: no missions or combat.

**Map and streaming.** Washdyke to Takapō is 100 to 105 km ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_State_Highway_8)): ~6 km compressed, plus ~5 km of basin loop (Takapō, canal, Pūkaki lookout, Twizel, Omarama, a dead-end spur towards the village with Aoraki as a backdrop model). Contiguous building would widen the world past the Alps backdrop, so make it a separately streamed region: own origin and terrain model, swapped behind the cutting at Burkes Pass. New tech, not just data: a lake terrain primitive (elevated water plane, shoreline), a canal channel, a dam landmark carrying a road, a `highcountry` tussock district type. Performance is the easiest of the three: open ground, few buildings, flat water quads, and the night sky makes Mount John a feature.

## Order of work and risks

Build in the order above. Oamaru is the cheap proof: every terrain primitive exists, 26 chunk rows, one new building archetype (whitestone). Dunedin is the content lift: a hill city, a campus, seven landmark kinds, and the grades-versus-physics question, worth prototyping first. The lakes are the tech lift: lakes, canals, dams and the region swap, on the lightest content.

Cross-cutting: `CityId`, `DistrictType`, terrain and landmark kinds live in lead-owned `src/core/types.ts`, so each contract change lands before its fan-out; the minimap atlas page and far mesh assume the current world box; saves must tolerate a grown map. Ngāi Tahu naming needs a Ngāi Tahu reader, not just the atlas (`RESEARCH.md` says the same), the lakes' spellings the open question. The content rules hold everywhere: real streets and landmarks, invented businesses and people, no real crimes or tragedies as plot, and Aoraki, the marae and Mount Cook village protected ground.
