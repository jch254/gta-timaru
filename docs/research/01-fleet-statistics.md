## 3a. Policy changes behind the used-import surge (official wording)

- 1990 Yearbook (URL above): "the introduction in December 1984 of the Motor Vehicle Industry Development Plan which exposed both assemblers and component manufacturers to increasing levels of import competition over the four years to 1988. By mid-1988 there were nine franchise holders assembling passenger cars in only seven separate plants."
- 1990 Yearbook: "In December 1987, following a review of the industry plan, the Government announced the removal of import controls from all automotive products from 1 January 1989 and a programme for reducing tariffs on both vehicles and components."
- 1990 Yearbook: "The 1970s saw the number of separate car assembly plants grow from ten to a high of 16, primarily as the result of the relaxation of restrictions on the importation of ckd packs."
- 1996 Yearbook: the industry "has coped with the adverse influences of domestic and international recession, together with the significant increase in used vehicles imported from Japan. Following falling sales in 1991 and 1992, sales of new vehicles stabilised in 1993. Sales increased significantly through 1994".
- Te Ara, "Cars and the motor industry" (https://teara.govt.nz/en/cars-and-the-motor-industry/print ; saved copy is a Wayback capture): "From less than 3,000 each year in 1985, imports had grown to 85,000 in 1990, and surged to more than 150,000 in 2004. Far more second-hand imports than new cars were being sold. Almost all had previously been registered in Japan, and 80% were Japanese makes – Toyotas made up 25%." (the 80%/25% figures describe the 2000s peak, not specifically 1995)
- Te Ara: "In the late 1980s the Motor Industry Development Plan led to reduced tariffs on imported vehicles ... Australian cars were already duty-free under the Closer Economic Relations agreement."
- Te Ara: "New car registrations reached 102,000 in 1973, the year of the first oil shock ... This number was not exceeded until 1989."

## 7. Derived estimates for a 1995 car mix (my arithmetic on the official figures above; NOT printed in any source)

Car fleet at 31 March 1995: 1,647,134 (plus 13,364 rentals, 4,458 taxis).
- Used imports registered 1987 to 1994: 12,129 + 17,372 + 50,966 + 85,324 + 47,351 + 39,146 + 43,841 + 62,088 = 358,217, about 22% of the car fleet at March 1995 if all survived (upper bound). Adding all of calendar 1995 (80,976) gives 439,193, about 26% by the end of 1995.
- NZ-new cars registered 1985 to 1994 (total minus ex-overseas): 81,516 + 76,075 + 77,499 + 71,217 + 83,862 + 74,422 + 55,615 + 52,964 + 53,822 + 61,765 = 688,757, about 42% of the fleet if all survived (upper bound).
- Remainder, NZ-new cars first registered before 1985 (more than 10 years old): at least about 600,000, about 36% of the fleet (lower bound, because any scrappage among the newer groups raises it). Many used imports were themselves built before 1985 (for example the 1982 Cresta above), so cars more than 10 years old by build date were plausibly 40% or more.
- Body of that older third: per sections 1b and 5 it is the 1972 to 1974 boom cohort (British and Australian designs: Kingswood, Cortina, Escort, Avenger/Hunter, Mini, Marina, Viva, Falcon, Valiant) plus the 1978 to 1984 NZ-assembled Japanese cohort (Corolla, Laser/323, Mirage, Sigma, Civic, Accord, Sunny, Bluebird, Cortina to 1983).
- New-car brand shares in the decade before 1995 (1985 to 1994 sums from table 1b, my arithmetic): Ford 129,684; Toyota 127,076; Mitsubishi 94,609; GM/Holden 76,149; Honda 63,200; Nissan 64,160; Mazda 38,630. Of 688,757 that is roughly Ford 19%, Toyota 18%, Mitsubishi 14%, Holden 11%, Nissan 9%, Honda 9%, Mazda 6%, all others 14%.
