# 04 Era research: economy, politics, sport, weather and the 1995 calendar
Canterbury and South Canterbury, c. 1990 to 1996. Compiled 18 Nov 2026 from pages opened this session or saved by the earlier attempt (raw4/). New downloads are in raw4b/. The uncut working notes with every quote are kept at raw4b/04-era-working-notes.md.

How to read this file
- Every item has a date, a URL and a verbatim quote from a page that was actually opened.
- "Y95" = NZ Official Yearbook 1995, https://www3.stats.govt.nz/New_Zealand_Official_Yearbooks/1995/NZOYB_1995.html
- "Y96" = NZ Official Yearbook 1996, https://www3.stats.govt.nz/New_Zealand_Official_Yearbooks/1996/NZOYB_1996.html (its "Chronology 1995" and "Weather in 1995" sections are the best single source found).
- Te Ara and NZHistory pages were read through web.archive.org copies (the live sites return 403 to automated fetches). URLs given are the live ones.
- "FLAG" marks deaths or real victims of crime or disaster. Weekdays in square brackets are calculated, not quoted.
- Access limits: Papers Past (The Press is online to 31 Dec 1995) blocks automated access, so nothing was read there; where a search-engine summary of a Papers Past article is all I have, it is listed under Unverified.

## 1. Freezing works and the meat industry

### Fortex Group collapse, March 1994 (Seafield near Ashburton, Silverstream near Mosgiel)
- 23 March 1994 [Wednesday]: receivers appointed. deListed NZ, https://www.delisted.co.nz (saved page raw4/delisted_fortex.html, "FORTEX GROUP LIMITED (FRX)"): "delisted following the appointment of KPMG Peat Marwick as receivers and managers on 23 March 1994". Delisting date on the page: "Delisted NZX 08/04/1994".
- Job losses, sources disagree:
  - Y95, Chronology 1994, March: "Meat processing group Fortex goes into receivership with the loss of 2,000 jobs in Tauranga, Ashburton and Mosgiel; farmers are owed $7.5 million."
  - Brian Easton, Listener 18 May 1996, https://www.eastonbh.ac.nz/1996/05/exfortex/ : "March 1994: Receiver called in. (It would appear that there are $90m of assets and $160m of liabilities). 1800 workers lose their jobs."
- Background from the same Easton article: "1985: Cattle Services, Fort Export, and Canterbury Venison merged to form Fortex, which owned a small processing plant in Seafield, Ashburton." / "1988, 1989: Pioneering agreements with workers which enabled the plant to run 22 hours a day, six days a week." / "November 1990: Fortex wins Company of the Year award" / "December 1990: Minister of Agriculture likens Fortex to Watties when opening the newly built Silverstream (Dunedin) plant (planned cost $35m, actual cost $50m)." / "April 1991: Fortex frequently commended by supporters of the Employments Contract Bill in parliament as an example of successful labour relations."
- FLAG (real crime, named people): same article: "Two years later its managing director ... was jailed for six and a half years." and "March 1996: ... found guilty on 12 fraud charges". The trial therefore ran in the background of 1995 to 1996. Use the collapse and the lost jobs and unpaid farmers as setting; do not use the individuals.
- Afterwards: ODT, https://www.odt.co.nz/news/dunedin/180-jobs-at-plant-in-doubt-vrzwa69n : "Silverstream was bought by SFF [then PPCS] following the collapse of Fortex in March 1994." Seafield: Wikipedia ANZCO Foods, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZCO_Foods , plant list: "Seafield, Canterbury | ANZCO Foods Canterbury (former Fortex Ashburton slaughter plant)"; and "ANZCO Foods started operating under the new ownership arrangement on 17 August 1995." (The date ANZCO took over Seafield was not found.)

### Weddel collapse, August 1994 (North Island, but the national mood)
- Y95, Chronology 1994, August: "Receivers move in to recover a $205 million debt by Weddell New Zealand and close meatworks at Whangarei, Cambridge, Gisborne, Hastings and Feilding with the loss of 2,300 jobs."

### How the work ran: the season, the chain, pay, the union
- Te Ara, https://teara.govt.nz/en/agricultural-processing-industries/print : "Freezing works operated on a highly seasonal basis – the 'killing season' started around August and reached a peak in January." / "From about 1930 'the chain' was introduced – which moves carcasses along as workers carried out a specific operation on them." / (solo slaughtermen) "were paid for each animal killed, with tongues used to count the tally."
- Same story, page 8: "Works were often double-shifted – two 12-hour shifts a day at the peak of the season, often with staff on a 'four days on, four days off' work schedule. Changes in employment legislation allowed for such arrangements, and brought a sharp decline in strikes. Striking meat workers lost $36 million in lost wages in 1990, the year before the Employment Contracts Act, and only $1 million the following season."
- Y96, production: "The New Zealand production season runs from 1 October to 30 September."
- Y96, farmers' side: "Producers can sell their stock on schedule, on a pool account system, on the hoof, on contract or by auction." Lamb schedule, Table 18.16 ($ a head including skin), PM grade 13.3 to 17.0 kg: 1990-91 "28.19", 1991-92 "29.19", 1992-93 "41.08", 1993-94 "41.32".
- Unions after 1991: see section 2.
