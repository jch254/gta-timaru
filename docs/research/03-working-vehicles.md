## D. Details that date a car (confirmed so far)

D1. Number plates. Wikipedia "Vehicle registration plates of New Zealand" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_New_Zealand (raw text saved working/wk/)
- Format: "From 1964 until March 2001 these number plates had two letters followed by one to four numbers (format LLnnnn)".
- Colours: "In November 1986, the plate design officially changed to black serials on a reflectorised white background ... The first official plate of this design had the serial MX100. Silver-on-black plates remain valid and in use".
- 1964 plates: "made of aluminium and had silver serials on a black background".
- Slashed zero: "In mid-1990, a slash was added to the zero character; the first plate to feature a slashed zero had the serial PC10."
- First series issued in each year (table in the article): 1980 JH, 1981 JY, 1982 KJ, 1983 KU, 1984 LO, 1985 LZ, 1986 MI, 1987 NA, 1988 NO, 1989 OC, 1990 OR, 1991 PM, 1992 RE, 1993 RS, 1994 SG, 1995 TB, 1996 TU, 1997 UU1000, 1998 WT, 1999 XR1000. (Consistent with the text: first white plate MX100 in November 1986.) Rule of thumb for 1995 traffic: plates before about MX are silver on black; MX onward are black on white.
- So a car first registered in 1995 carries a plate from about TB to TT; 1994 SG to TA; brand-new in early 1996 TU onward. No letter Q as first letter: "the RA series thus followed the PZ series in 1991". Letter V not used after 1971.
- Digits: until late 1996 series started from 1, so short plates like "TI906" exist ("From late 1996 ... each series ... started from 1000 instead of from 1 ... The UR series was the first such series").
- Three-letter format: "A new system began in April 2001, with three letters (starting with AAA) followed by three numbers starting at 100." Not valid for 1995.
- Personalised plates: the article says CROWN "was issued in 1986, predating all other personalised plates by two years", i.e. personalised plates from 1988. Also 1990 commemorative plates "format nnnnNZ, with the lettering in red on white".
- Photo cross-check: police Commodore "TI906" captioned 1994 by the photo site vs table start of TB in 1995. Minor disagreement (the car may be a 1994 build first registered 1995).

## D (continued)

D2. Open road speed limit. NZ Official Yearbook 1996 (Statistics NZ) https://www3.stats.govt.nz/New_Zealand_Official_Yearbooks/1996/NZOYB_1996.html (saved working/yb/yb1996.txt; same text in the 1995 edition): "Speed limits. The maximum speed limits for highways and motorways are: 100 km/h for cars, motorcycles, vans and light vehicles; 90 km/h for buses, heavy motor and articulated vehicles; and 80 km/h for school buses and any vehicles towing trailers. A general speed limit of 50 km/h is fixed in all closely populated areas. Areas with a speed limit of 70 km/h may also be specified".
- GAME USE: trucks and coaches on SH1 legally 90, anything towing a trailer or caravan 80, cars 100. Towns 50, some fringes 70. Also: "limited speed zones" (LSZ signs) existed.
- Speed cameras were new: same yearbook, "Following the introduction of the speed camera programme there was a significant reduction in average traffic speeds." (ties to A4, the 1994 unmarked Telstar camera wagon).
- Wikipedia "Speed limits in New Zealand" (saved fetch/wp_speed.txt): "Between metrication in 1975 and 1985, 80 km/h was the open road limit."

All vehicles using the roads must be inspected regularly ... They are inspected every six months but vehicles first registered since 1 August 1987 and less than six years old may be inspected every 12 months. Most lightweight vehicles are required to have a 'Warrant of Fitness' which can be issued at approved garages, or at testing stations operated by local authorities. All heavy vehicles ... Certificate of Fitness ... Taxicabs and rental vehicles also require a Certificate of Fitness. From 1994 Vehicle Inspection Certificates (VIC) have been phased in to replace Certificate of Fitness and Warrant of Fitness stickers. D4. Registration (licence) label. Yearbook 1996: "Licence label. A new-style label introduced in 1995 is displayed inside the vehicle's windscreen. The registration number, make, model and year of the vehicle; and month and year of the label's expiry are verified by a barcode on the label. The label also shows when the vehicle was first registered in New Zealand, whether road user charges and a Vehicle Inspection Certificate are required, and the label number and date it was printed."
- Annual relicensing charge, ordinary car: "$197.47" (1995 edition) and "$157.75" (1996 edition). Vehicles "are relicensed progressively throughout the year".
- GAME USE: 1995 is the changeover year, so both an older-style label and the new printed barcode label are defensible. Appearance of the pre-1995 label: Unverified.

D6. Fleet mix that dates the streets. Yearbook 1996 Table 23.1: used imports ("Cars previously registered overseas") were 12,129 in 1987, 85,324 in 1990, 47,351 in 1991, 39,146 in 1992, 43,841 in 1993, 62,088 in 1994, out of total first registrations of 89,628 / 159,746 / 102,966 / 92,110 / 97,663 / 123,853.
