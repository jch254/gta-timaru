## Early confirmed items (from saved files; filed under headings below when the full document is assembled)

- LIQUOR LAW (sections 1, 2). Te Ara, "Liquor laws" (Paul Christoffel), http://www.TeAra.govt.nz/en/liquor-laws/print (saved copy fetch/teara_liquor-laws.txt).
  - Six o'clock closing ended 1967; drinking age 20 from 1969: "In 1967 another referendum on licensing hours convinced Parliament to end 50 years of six o'clock closing. The drinking age was lowered to 20 in 1969."
  - 1989 Act (the regime in force in 1995): "The new act allowed for just four types of licence ... Bars no longer needed to provide overnight accommodation to avoid hefty turnover taxes. ... resulting in several thousand new bars and licensed eating places. Supermarkets were allowed to sell wine."
  - So in 1995: wine but NOT beer in supermarkets, no Sunday liquor sales from shops, drinking age 20. Changed 1999: "Supermarkets and grocery stores were allowed to sell beer as well as wine, liquor sales were permitted on Sundays (excluding Easter and Christmas) and the drinking age was lowered to 18."
- GAMBLING (sections 5, 7). Te Ara, "Gambling" (saved copy fetch/teara_gambling.txt, https://teara.govt.nz/en/gambling/print).
  - Lotto: "On the day of the first draw (22 July 1987) crowds packed Lotto outlets"; "By August 1989 the country had more than 1,000 Lotto outlets".
  - Instant Kiwi: "the Lotteries Commission introduced Instant Kiwi scratch cards in 1989 ... there being a one-in-seven chance of winning a prize."; Golden Kiwi "terminated ... in 1989". "In April 1993 Lotto Strike was introduced."
  - Pokies: "In 1988 the government legalised what New Zealanders colloquially called 'pokies' ... for clubs and hotels."
  - Casino: "Christchurch opened the first in November 1994. Auckland followed with the SkyCity casino in January 1996".
  - TAB: "In 1996 betting on a wide range of sports was introduced at the TAB. The TAB's racing television channel Trackside ... began in 1992." (So in 1995 the TAB was racing only: horses and dogs.) Housie "legalised in 1959".

## 6. How people talked; phones

### 6a. Phones

- 03 area code and seven digits. Wikipedia "Telephone numbers in New Zealand" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_numbers_in_New_Zealand : "Between 1989 and 1993, the New Zealand Phone Number Update standardised New Zealand's telephone numbering plan. The number of STD codes was reduced from about 80 to just five (03, 04, 06, 07, and 09), and local numbers were standardised to seven digits. In many areas, seven-digit local numbers were created by prefixing additional digits to the existing shorter numbers". Before that: "six-digit numbers began to be introduced in ... Christchurch from 1964"; "the Halswell exchange was incorporated into the 03 area code as 03 22". The old STD table on the page lists e.g. "Woodbury | WRY | 056 32" (South Canterbury was 056 before 03).
- Party lines: "Multi-party lines were identified by a suffix letter corresponding to the subscriber's ring pattern in Morse code. For example, subscriber 193M was signalled by two long rings, while 193S was signalled by three short rings."; "manual exchanges remained in use in some areas until 1990." Rotary dials: "numbered in reverse compared with most other countries, with 9 closest to the finger stop".
- 025 mobiles: "025 | Unused | 6 or 7 digits | Was used by Telecom New Zealand until it was shut down on 31 March 2007"; "In the late 1990s however, Telecom mobile phones could dial other Telecom mobile phones without the (then) 025 prefix".
