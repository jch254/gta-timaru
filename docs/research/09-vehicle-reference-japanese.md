# Japanese vehicle reference specs for Canterbury 1995

All figures are from saved Wikipedia wikitext (wiki-raw/) or pages fetched in this session. "not found" means the page did not give the figure.

Conventions: Wikipedia power figures quoted in PS or hp are shown with an arithmetic conversion to kW (1 PS = 0.7355 kW, 1 hp = 0.7457 kW), marked "conv.". Dimensions given in inches on the page are converted and marked "conv. from in". Recognition notes are only those stated in page text; where the page text gives no description the entry says "not described on page".

## NZ assembly background (applies to many entries)

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_New_Zealand and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_New_Zealand

- Toyota: CKD cars assembled by Steel Motor Assemblies, Christchurch (first Corona Feb 1967; later "Toyota New Zealand Christchurch", listed as "Christchurch (commercial)", closed Sept 1996) and Campbell Motor Industries, Thames (first Corolla April 1968; later "Toyota New Zealand Thames", closed Oct 1998). Locally assembled models listed (flagged citation needed on the page): Starlet, Corolla, Corona, Crown, Cressida, Hilux, LiteAce, Land Cruiser, HiAce.
- Nissan/Datsun: assembled by contract plants (Campbell Industries Thames: 1200, 1600, 120Y, 180B; Motor Holdings Waitara: 1200 wagon, 120Y wagon; Todd Motors Porirua: 180B; Nissan plant at Mount Roskill from 25 March 1974: 1200, 120Y) until Nissan's own plant at Wiri, south Auckland (late 1970s; closed July 1998).
- Honda: New Zealand Motor Corporation first built Hondas at Petone in 1976, then Mount Wellington, consolidating at Stoke, Nelson (closed 21 August 1998).
- Mitsubishi: Todd Motors, Petone then Todd Park, Porirua (assembly from early 1974; sold to Mitsubishi 1987; closed June 1998).
- Mazda: B-series utes first built by Steel Bros, Christchurch, 1969; cars from 1972 by Motor Holdings, Otahuhu (last vehicle a Mazda utility in 1987), then shared with Ford at Vehicle Assemblers of New Zealand (VANZ), Wiri/Manukau (1987 to March 1997).
- Suzuki: Wanganui plant (closed 1988). GM New Zealand also assembled some Isuzus and Suzukis in the 1980s.
- Used imports: "Imports rose from less than 3,000 cars in 1985 to 85,000 in 1990 ... Second-hand Japanese cars made up the majority of these cars."

# "Boy racer" cars, 1993 to 1996

What the sources opened in this session actually say:

- Te Ara (https://teara.govt.nz/en/photograph/27019/boy-racers): "'Boy racers' were youths who were into cars in the 1990s and the early 21st century. In the 1970s and 1980s they would have been termed 'hoons'. ... Since the 1990s cheap Japanese second-hand car imports have meant that cars are much more affordable for young people." Note the dating: for a 1995 setting, "hoon" and "petrolhead" are the older terms and "boy racer" is the newer 1990s one.
- Wikipedia Boy racer (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_racer): "Most cheap vehicles in New Zealand are used Japanese imports and the culture follows modification of these cars." The "Boy Racer Act" is 2003 and the first crushed car (2012) was a white Nissan Laurel: these are later-era facts, not 1995.
- Wikipedia Automotive industry in New Zealand: used imports "rose from less than 3,000 cars in 1985 to 85,000 in 1990", mostly second-hand Japanese cars.
- Wikipedia Nissan Bluebird (910): Japanese-market wing (guard) mounted mirrors "distinguished thousands of used cars imported from Japan into NZ in the late 1980s and early 90's" (a good visual tell for an import in the game).
- Auto Trader NZ, "1992 Mazda Familia GT-R", Alex Schultz, 21 Aug 2021 (https://autotrader.co.nz/classics/1992-mazda-familia-gt-r): the BF Familia/323 "had a cult following here, the 'DOHC Turbo' a very popular rocket in the early days of Japanese imports"; Japanese Group A homologation specials "used to be a relatively common sight on New Zealand roads" as "turbocharged AWD specials flowed across our borders freely thanks to our relaxed import laws ... and they were cheap too".
- NZ Classic Car, "Last Tango in the Fast Lane", Gerard Richards, 30 Apr 2026 (https://classiccar.co.nz/?p=21771): first-person account of buying a 1982 six-cylinder Skyline Turbo coupe about 1990, seeing a 1985 R30 Skyline RS FJ20 Turbo at the Manukau City Car Fair about 1996, and buying a Toyota Sprinter twin-cam coupe and an imported Honda CRX twin-cam about 1996.

Conclusion: no source opened gives an explicit list of "the stereotypical boy racer cars of 1993 to 1996" (not found). What the sources do support for that window is 1980s-build turbo and twin-cam imports, roughly 6 to 12 years old at the time: Mazda Familia/323 BF "DOHC Turbo" 4WD, early Skyline turbos (R30, and by build date R31), Toyota Sprinter/Levin twin cams, Honda CR-X and Civic twin cams, plus the Group A 4WD turbo specials (Familia GT-X/GT-R, Galant VR-4, Legacy RS by the Auto Trader description of the class).
